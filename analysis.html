1 What this tool actually is

In one sentence: it's a weather forecaster for the Bitcoin price.

The weather analogy. A weather app doesn't know if it'll rain tomorrow. It looks at today's temperature, wind, humidity and pressure, compares that to thousands of past days, and says "70% chance of rain." This tool does exactly the same thing — but instead of clouds and wind, it watches the Bitcoin market, and instead of "rain," it predicts whether the price will go up, down, or stay flat over the next 1 to 15 minutes.

It runs as two parts working together:

  • A backend (the engine, written in Python) — it pulls in live market data, does the maths, and makes the forecasts.
  • A frontend (the dashboard, what you see in the browser) — charts, numbers, and the up/down/flat predictions, refreshing live.

It makes forecasts for six different time windows at once — 1, 3, 5, 7, 10 and 15 minutes ahead — because a "this minute" signal and a "next quarter hour" signal are different questions.

2 How the machine works, step by step

Think of it as a factory assembly line. Raw data goes in one end, a polished forecast comes out the other. Here are the stations on the line.

1

Collect the raw data data_ingestion.py

Connects live to Binance (the world's biggest crypto exchange) and listens to every trade, every change in the order book, and every new price candle. It also calls Coinbase (a big US exchange) and Bybit (a big derivatives exchange), plus a "Fear & Greed" mood index. If a connection drops, it automatically reconnects.

2

Measure the "order flow" order_flow.py

This is the tool's edge over a basic charting app. Instead of only looking at price, it watches the pressure behind the price: are buyers or sellers more aggressive right now? Are there big walls of buy/sell orders waiting? How fast are trades happening? Are any "whales" (large traders) active?

3

Turn everything into numbers features.py

Computers can't read "the market feels bullish." So this station converts everything into 40 tidy numbers — classic chart indicators (RSI, MACD, etc.), the order-flow pressure readings, derivatives data, and a few "combination" numbers. Every number is squeezed into a small range so no single one drowns out the others.

4

Read the "weather regime" regime.py

The market behaves differently in different moods. This station labels the current environment as Trending Up, Trending Down, Range (going sideways), High Volatility (wild) or Low Volatility (calm). A strategy that works in a calm market fails in a wild one, so knowing the regime matters.

5

Make the forecast model.py

The 40 numbers (plus the last hour of history) are fed to four prediction models that each vote up / down / flat. Their votes are blended into one answer per time window. Covered in detail in the next section.

6

Grade past forecasts prediction_verifier.py + backtester.py

Every forecast is remembered. Once its time window passes, the tool checks: was it right? This running report card is shown live and — crucially — fed back so the system can adjust itself.

7

Serve it to the screen server.py

The conductor of the orchestra. Every 2 seconds it gathers the latest of everything, runs the forecasts, updates the report card, and pushes the whole bundle to your browser dashboard.

The "triple-barrier" trick. Most simple tools ask "will the price be higher in 5 minutes?" This one asks a smarter, more realistic question: "will the price hit my profit target before it hits my stop-loss?" Imagine a race between a finish line above the price and a trap-door below it — whichever gets touched first is the answer. This matches how a real trader actually wins or loses money, not just where the price happens to land at the buzzer.

3 The four "brains" that vote

No single model is trusted on its own. Four very different ones each give an opinion, and the system blends them — like a panel of advisors instead of one guru.

The panel of advisors

  • XGBoost — the star analyst. Brilliant at spotting complex, tangled patterns. Gets the biggest vote (~35%).
  • LightGBM — same family as XGBoost but faster; a strong second opinion (~25%).
  • Random Forest — the steady, cautious one. Rarely brilliant, rarely disastrous; keeps the panel grounded (~20%).
  • Logistic Regression — the simple, old-school advisor. A sanity check so the clever ones don't over-think a situation that's actually simple (~10%).

Two clever touches most hobby tools skip

Honesty calibration. A lot of prediction tools cry wolf — they say "90% sure!" when they're really right 60% of the time. This tool runs a correction (called isotonic calibration) so that when it says 70%, it has historically been right about 70% of the time. That makes the confidence number actually mean something.
Anti-flicker locking. Without a brake, the forecast would flip "UP! DOWN! UP!" every couple of seconds and be useless. So each time window locks its answer for a sensible period (the 1-minute view holds ~55 seconds, the 15-minute view holds ~15 minutes) and only flips early if the opposite signal becomes overwhelmingly strong for several readings in a row. It's a stubbornness dial that prevents nervous twitching.

On top of the four models, the final answer also mixes in five "gut-feel" signals: order-flow pressure, derivatives positioning, plain chart reading, market mood, and whale activity. The models get the majority say (~55%); these add colour.

4 How it grades and teaches itself

This is the most genuinely advanced part of the whole tool — and the easiest to appreciate without any trading knowledge.

A

It keeps a report card

Every prediction is logged with a timestamp. When its time window elapses, the tool compares prediction vs. what actually happened and marks it a hit or miss — split out by time window, by direction (was it better at calling ups or downs?), and by how confident it was.

B

It rewards whoever is hot right now

If Random Forest has been the most accurate this hour, its vote automatically gets bigger. If a model goes cold, its vote shrinks. The panel re-weights itself based on live results, not just old test scores.

C

It retrains when it's slipping

If accuracy for a time window is both low and getting worse, the system flags itself and re-studies the recent data from scratch. It also automatically retrains every 30 minutes regardless, to stay current.

D

It adjusts its own caution

When it's doing badly it turns up the "smoothing" (becomes more conservative, changes its mind less). When it's doing well it loosens up. It tunes its own personality based on results.

In plain terms: it's a student who marks their own homework after every test, gives more trust to whichever study method is currently working, and re-reads the textbook the moment grades start to drop. That feedback loop is rare in hobby projects and is the system's strongest feature.

6 The Coinbase + Bybit upgrade ("Phase 6")

The plan was to look beyond one exchange. Here's the idea in plain English — and a surprise about its status.

Surprise: it's already built. The plan describes Phase 6 as "proposed," but the code already has it. There are now 40 features (up from 38), a working Coinbase connection, and a Bybit data fetcher running on a 30-second timer. So this is less "should we do it" and more "let's finish and polish it."

The two new ideas, explained simply

1. Coinbase Premium

Coinbase is where many US institutions buy Bitcoin. When the price on Coinbase runs slightly higher than on Binance, it often means big American money is buying aggressively — frequently before the rest of the market catches up. So this gap is an early-warning signal of serious buying or selling.

2. Global Open Interest (Binance + Bybit)

"Open Interest" = how much money is currently bet in the futures market. Watching it across two big exchanges instead of one gives a fuller picture of how leveraged (and therefore how fragile) the market is.

Two expert tweaks worth adding (and both are genuinely high-value):
  • Premium velocity — don't just track the gap, track how fast it's changing. "+$18 and rising fast" is a far stronger signal than the +$18 alone.
  • OI divergence — when Binance and Bybit's bets move in opposite directions, that disagreement is often more predictive than either number on its own.

Answers to the plan's open questions

  • Is 30-second polling OK? Yes. Funding and open-interest data change slowly; 30 seconds is comfortably fresh and safe on rate limits.
  • Track the Binance-vs-Bybit OI difference separately? Yes — that "divergence" is exactly the kind of signal worth having.
  • Combine or show exchanges separately in the UI? Show them separately (Binance OI, Bybit OI, Global OI, and the gap). Seeing the disagreement is the whole point.

Why The single biggest remaining accuracy gain. The same signal means opposite things in a calm market vs. a panicking one.

large for crypto

fewer bad calls

Why Pros often gain more from skipping the weak signals than from improving the strong ones. Knowing when to stay out is half the game.

strong for 1–3 min

8 Honest reality check (and real bugs I found)

A fair tool review names the problems too. These are grounded in the actual code, explained plainly.

Real issues found in the code

Bug — two time windows have no real brain. confirmed
The tool shows forecasts for 1, 3, 5, 7, 10 and 15 minutes. But it only ever trains models for 1, 5, 10 and 15. The 3-minute and 7-minute forecasts have no trained model behind them — they fall back to the simpler "gut-feel" signals only. (Confirmed: the saved-models folder contains files for 1, 5, 10, 15 — none for 3 or 7.) Fix: train all six windows, or stop displaying the two untrained ones as equals.
Bug — the star analyst is mostly missing. confirmed
XGBoost is described as the primary model (biggest vote). But only xgb_1.pkl exists on disk — the 5/10/15-minute windows have no saved XGBoost, so they quietly run on the other three models. The system is designed to degrade gracefully, so it doesn't crash — but it's silently running below its intended strength. Fix: investigate why XGBoost isn't training/saving for those windows.
Weak spot — "approximate" history in testing. improve
When it grades itself on historical data, it doesn't have the true high/low of each past minute, so it fakes them (price ±0.1%). The clever "race to the target" labelling is therefore running on rough estimates, which makes the accuracy scores less trustworthy than they look. Fix: feed the real high/low values, which it already collects, into the grader.
Weak spot — some data is collected but barely used. improve
Bybit's funding rate is fetched but never turned into a signal. "Funding velocity" is a listed feature but is hard-wired to zero. Liquidation data is downloaded but only shown on screen, never fed to the models. There's collected-but-unused value sitting on the table.
Weak spot — it learns from very little data. improve
It retrains on roughly the last 1,000 one-minute candles (~16 hours). That's a thin slice of history to learn from, and it can make the models jumpy. More history (or smarter sampling) would steady them.

What's genuinely good

  • The self-grading + self-teaching loop is the real standout — most projects at this level don't have it.
  • Honesty calibration on the confidence numbers shows real maturity.
  • The anti-flicker locking is a thoughtful, practical touch that makes the output usable.
  • Order-flow awareness already puts it well ahead of a basic "draw some indicators" charting app.
  • Clean, modular code — each job lives in its own file, so all the upgrades above are realistic to add.
The unavoidable truth. Predicting Bitcoin 1–15 minutes ahead is extraordinarily hard. The honest ceiling for a tool like this is being right modestly more often than chance — and even that is valuable and difficult. The gap to a top-tier fund isn't a missing feature; it's proprietary ultra-fast data, execution infrastructure, and years of research. Treat this as a serious learning platform and a solid research base, not a money printer.

9 Glossary — every term, in plain English

If a word in this page (or the app) confused you, it's defined here. No prior knowledge assumed.

Bitcoin / BTC / BTCUSDTBTC is Bitcoin. "BTCUSDT" just means "the price of Bitcoin measured in (a) US dollars" — the thing the tool predicts.
Exchange (Binance, Coinbase, Bybit)Online marketplaces where people buy and sell crypto. The tool listens to three of the biggest.
Order bookThe live list of all the buy and sell orders waiting to be filled. Its shape hints at which way price may move.
Order flowThe pressure behind the price — are buyers or sellers being more aggressive right now.
CVD (Cumulative Volume Delta)A running tally of aggressive buying minus aggressive selling. Rising = buyers in control.
Order Book Imbalance (OBI)Whether there are more buy orders or sell orders stacked up. More buys = mild upward hint.
SpreadThe tiny gap between the best buy price and best sell price. A wide gap signals nervous, thin trading.
WhaleA trader placing very large orders. Their moves can shove the price around, so the tool watches them.
VolatilityHow wild and jumpy the price is. High volatility = big fast swings; low = calm.
Open Interest (OI)The total amount of money currently bet in the futures market. High OI = lots of leverage = more fragile.
Funding rateA small recurring fee between futures traders that reveals whether the crowd is leaning bullish or bearish.
Long / Short"Long" = betting price goes up. "Short" = betting it goes down. The long/short ratio shows which way the crowd leans.
LiquidationWhen an over-leveraged trader's bet is force-closed by the exchange. Waves of these cause violent price cascades.
Coinbase PremiumHow much higher (or lower) Bitcoin trades on Coinbase vs Binance — a tell for big US institutional buying/selling.
Derivatives / FuturesContracts to bet on Bitcoin's price without owning it. This is where most of the leverage (and risk) lives.
FeatureA single number describing the market that the AI reads. This tool reads 40 of them at once.
Indicator (RSI, MACD, ADX, ATR…)Classic formulas that summarise price/volume into one number — e.g. RSI hints at "overbought," ADX measures trend strength, ATR measures volatility.
ModelA piece of AI that has studied past data and makes predictions. This tool uses four different ones together.
EnsembleCombining several models so their blended answer beats any single one — the "panel of advisors."
Confidence / CalibrationHow sure the tool is (e.g. 70%). "Calibration" makes sure that 70% really means right-7-times-in-10.
HorizonHow far ahead a forecast looks — 1, 3, 5, 7, 10 or 15 minutes here.
RegimeThe market's current "mood": trending, ranging, calm or wild. Strategy should change with it.
BacktestReplaying history to check how the strategy would have done — a practice exam on past data.
Triple-barrier labellingDefining "correct" as hitting a profit target before a stop-loss — the realistic, trader-style way to score a prediction.
Stop-loss / Take-profitPre-set exit prices: one caps your loss, the other locks in your gain.
Sharpe ratio / Win rate / Profit factorReport-card stats: Sharpe = return vs. riskiness; win rate = % of winning calls; profit factor = total wins ÷ total losses.
Fear & Greed IndexA 0–100 gauge of overall market emotion. Extreme fear or greed often marks turning points.
