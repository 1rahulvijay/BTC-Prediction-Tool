⚡ The 30-second version

  1. BTC direction is a coin-flip. Permanently. No model, grade, or indicator in this app picks UP/DOWN better than ~50%. Anything that claims to is context, not a signal.
  2. The one measured edge: in the final ~30 seconds of a 5m round, the market slightly underprices whichever side is already winning. Buy that leader at its real ask, hold to settlement. That's the whole strategy.
  3. It's a structural average, not round-picking: nothing — not P(Hold), not BTC state, not a 1,500-feature model — can tell WHICH rounds are better. So the rule fires on every qualifying round, identically.
  4. The app's job is discipline, not cleverness: right market, right seconds, real fresh ask, sane price, one entry. The app does this automatically on paper and shows you the ledger.
  5. Nothing is real-money approved. The rule must survive ≥500 live settled rounds with positive fee-adjusted EV first. The 📜 tile tracks that gate live.

👉 Which side, and from what price?

Side = the LEADER. Never a forecast. Every card names it: "▲ UP leads $23".

  • Live price above the price-to-beat → UP is the leader → the UP share is the candidate.
  • Live price below the price-to-beat → DOWN is the leader → the DOWN share is the candidate.

Price = the live ask on that leader share, shown on every card in the LIVE POLYMARKET SHARES block (Buy = ask you'd pay · Sell now = bid you'd get · size = shares offered). The card's numbers ARE the real order book — refreshed every second, discarded if stale (>5s) or from the wrong round.

Worked example. 5m round, 27s left, price $23 above the line → UP leads. UP ask = 84¢. Taker fee ≈ 0.07·0.84·0.16 ≈ 1¢. If UP holds: +100 − 84 − 1 = +15¢ profit. If it flips: −85¢. The rule takes this bet every time it appears — because on average across hundreds of rounds, the 84¢ ask underprices the ~86% hold-rate. Same round with ask 55¢ → SKIP (below 60¢ the underpricing disappears into coin-flip noise).

🧪 Why so simple — the evidence that killed everything else

The strategy is one line because four independent tests all reached the same verdict: the ask already contains everything knowable about the round.

TestQuestionVerdict
Champion-gate lift (shuffled-gate nulls)Do BTC-state gates ($20-lead, TREND, 15m-agreement) improve EV?NO — they raise win% but the ask rises faster (p 0.74–0.97)
Setup-quality filtersDo P(Hold)/grade filters find better rounds?NO — they just select into pricier asks
Early-window strategiesIs there edge before the final minute?NO — the book is efficient early; every early entry ≈ 0 or negative
Complexity wall (ridge/poly/1,500 random features)Can a large model rank rounds?NO — shuffled-label null trades at the SAME EV as the real model

The conclusion: the edge is a structural intercept — a constant few-cent average underpricing of the late leader — not conditional alpha. You cannot pick the good rounds; you can only take all of them at sane prices and let the average work. That's why the rule is frozen and why the app shows discipline checks, not "this round is special" scores.

✅ The 9 checks — the app's actual job

Since no model picks rounds, everything that matters is operational. The app verifies these automatically for every paper entry; if you ever act manually, this is the entire checklist:

1. Is it a 5m round? (15m = separate shadow, separate evidence) 2. Is it the final 20–32 seconds? (earlier = no edge, later = no fill) 3. Who is the current leader? (higher bid — not the model, not your opinion) 4. Is the ask ≥ 60¢ and < 97¢? (below 60¢ = coin-flip zone, above = no room) 5. Is the quote FRESH? (≤5s old, exact round — stale quote = no trade) 6. Is there size at the ask? (the card shows it) 7. Is latency acceptable? (a human clicking is ~1–3s behind the logged quote) 8. Is the settlement join working? (recorder live on the tile) 9. ONE entry per round. (no re-entries, no doubling)

Any check fails → no trade → that IS the system working. Most rounds produce no entry; the rule's denominator counts them honestly.

💀 Never do these — measured, not opinions

TemptationWhy it feels rightWhat it measured

Take profit early (+20–50%, else settle)"lock in gains"−1.8 to −4.4¢ — 78% of TPs HIT and it still loses: each TP caps a winner headed to $1, losers still ride to $0

Bet the direction model"the AI says UP"~50% forever — every model family, every window, every retrain
Wait for a "better" round (grades/setups)"be selective"Selectivity selects into pricier asks — EV falls, not rises
Buy cheap leaders early (<60¢)"more upside"The market is efficient early; cheapness = real flip risk, correctly priced
Bet the losing side to revert (fade)"it'll bounce back"Retracted look-ahead leak; causal fade wins ~42% — below breakeven

The one law: every extra spread crossing and every early exit is a transfer to the market maker. The only measured leak is the final ~30 seconds, crossed once, held to settlement.

🧠 Honest math

  • Cents ≈ percent of stake. 1 paper share of a $1 market: +2.5¢ EV = +2.5% per trade. This is a volume edge — small, frequent, real only in aggregate.
  • Win rate is not profit. The live ledger already demonstrated 82.9% wins with a negative total — a few high-ask losers outweigh many small winners. Read EV and PF, never win%.
  • n < 100 per strategy = noise. The rule's own bar is n≥500. Nothing before the gate is evidence; everything before the gate is weather.
  • BTC prices in the app are PYTH (the settlement oracle), not Binance — they routinely differ by tens of dollars. Don't compare the blotter to TradingView.
  • Exits gap. Stops and TPs sell into the visible bid, which can be pulled — a −3¢ stop can fill at −20¢. That's the market, honestly accounted, and one more reason the rule holds to settlement instead.
  • Paper fills assume 1 share at top-of-book with ~0 latency. Real clicking is slower; real size slips. This gap is exactly what the live gate exists to measure before any real money.
  • Not financial advice. Educational decision-support only. Never bet money you can't afford to lose.
