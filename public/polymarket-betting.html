⚡ The 30-second version

  1. Only bet when source = MODEL lean (never ⚠ WEAK / fallback — those are coin-flips, measured).
  2. If the lean is FLIPPING (changing UP↔DOWN every minute) → SKIP. A flip means the model is near-50/50; instability is the no-edge signal. Stable conviction = real signal. why →
  3. The magnitude must project PAST the line — "expects drop of $40 → projects $30 below beat" is real; "$8 drop, $20 to the line" is not. (It's a typical move, not a guarantee.)
  4. Skip CHOP / STRETCH outlooks, and skip Grade C.
  5. The bet only profits if your win-rate > the share price you pay. 60% wins at 65¢ loses money.

Reality check: 5m BTC direction is ~coin-flip for almost everyone. The edge is selectivity — bet only the few stable, high-grade, magnitude-clears windows; sit out the rest (which is most of them). Fewer bets, higher win rate. That's the whole game.

Final ~30s of a 5m round → buy the CURRENT LEADER at its ask → skip asks under 60c → one entry per round → hold to settlement.

✅ How to take action — the actual workflow

Default = NO BET. On 5m/15m BTC, direction is a coin-flip — live: 49.8% over 8,039 settled rounds. Sitting out is the correct action on ~90%+ of windows; WAIT/AVOID are wins, not failures. You are hunting the rare window where the market misprices a calibrated edge — not trying to call every move.

👉 Which side (UP or DOWN), and from what price?

Side = the LEADER. Not a direction forecast. The Round State card names it for you — "Leader: UP leads $23":

  • Live price is above the price-to-beat → UP is the leader → you buy the UP share.
  • Live price is below the price-to-beat → DOWN is the leader → you buy the DOWN share.

You are betting "the side already ahead stays ahead" — which is exactly what P(Hold) measures. You do NOT use the ensemble UP/DOWN arrow (that's the coin-flip, shown only as context). The old "buy the losing side / fade" play is retracted — don't do it.

From what price = the live ask on that leader share, and only when it's cheap enough. The entry point is the current ask in cents, gated by:

Buy the LEADER share only when ALL are true: • 15–120 seconds left • leader is ≥ $10 from the price-to-beat line • P(Hold) ≥ 93% • a FRESH live ask exists (≤ 5s old) ← requires the recorder running • the ask is cheap enough that: min(P(Hold), 0.91) − ask − taker_fee − 0.03 buffer > required_edge → the card then shows Decision = PAPER and Execution = PAPER EDGE

Worked example. 5m round, 70s left, price is $23 above the line → UP is the leader. P(Hold)=94% (fair value capped at 91c). Live UP ask = 84c. Taker fee ≈ 0.07·0.84·0.16 ≈ 1c; buffer 3c → 91 − 84 − 1 − 3 = +3c edge → PAPER: record a paper buy of the UP share at 84c. Same setup but UP ask = 89c → 91 − 89 − 1 − 3 = −2c → NO EDGE: right side, too expensive → no bet. The side is decided by who leads; the bet/no-bet is decided by the ask price.

Exit: hold to settlement (the leader share pays $1 if it holds, $0 if it flips), or cut earlier if the recorder's bid no longer covers your cost and Flip risk jumps. Never enter without a fresh live ask — a model target, midpoint, or stale quote is not a tradeable price.

The 6 steps (stop at the first one that fails)

  1. Market is real. Only 5m & 15m are bettable. 1/3/7/10m are practice mirrors — never fund them.
  2. Use the already-ahead side, not a fresh direction forecast. The 5m/15m ensemble is currently below 50% on resolved live direction, so it is context only. A late P(Hold) setup asks whether the side already ahead will remain ahead.
  3. P(Hold) clears the structural gate. Require 15–120 seconds left, at least $10 from the line, and calibrated P(Hold) ≥ 0.93. Count only the first qualifying entry in each round.
  4. Feed and market quote are fresh. The exact round must have an executable ask no more than five seconds old. A midpoint, model target, stale quote, or missing order book is not a price you can trade.
  5. THE GATE — the only reason to place money. The champion flags a PAPER_BET candidate only when:
    fair_value − ask − costs − buffer > required_edge
    In plain terms: the live Polymarket share (ask) is cheaper than the model's fair value by more than the safety buffer. A perfect-looking setup with a rich ask reads NO_EDGE → no bet. The model is never the edge by itself; the mispriced market price is.
  6. Paper-track until independently proven. Record the actual ask, fill, fee, outcome, and return. No real-money sizing is justified by the current sample.

🛤️ The path engine — how to trade the window (any direction)

On the corrected causal test the fade wins only ~42% (Wilson LB 40%), below the 50% breakeven, and loses money even on the BTC proxy before Polymarket costs. The fade is NOT a deployable edge. The strongest real instrument is conditional P(Hold), and even that only profits when conservative P(Hold) − ask − fee − slippage − buffer > 0. Treat the sections below as research/paper only. This is a research instrument, not a validated market edge. Direction (which way it closes) is a coin-flip — so you never bet the side from this model alone. The path engine instead forecasts the shape of the window: will it move, will it CHOP or TREND, will it touch an extreme early. All of that is measured predictable out-of-sample (AUC 0.75–0.91). Polymarket lets you bet either side and exit on share-price moves, so you trade the path: fade extremes in chop, ride trends, skip quiet.

👀 Watch the card — the fade in 4 steps:
  1. 🕐 reversal window — is it STRONG? (favorable time = more/better setups)
  2. ▶ PLAY — is it FADE-SETUP? (active chop, extremes likely)
  3. 🎯 TOUCHED fires → record a paper fade candidate; do not treat P(reach anchor) as binary-share fair value.
  4. Capture the actual entry ask, future exit bid, fees, and stop value. Without those, a BTC return to the anchor does not prove a profitable share trade.

Skip if 🕐 WEAK, PLAY = SKIP/RIDE, P(reach anchor) < 45%, or the touch is LATE. And for real money: only when the share ask is mispriced (the gate). Everything is PAPER until the recorder proves it.

The fields behind the play (all calibrated)

  • ROUND-TRIP % + style — CHOP (visits both sides) vs TREND. Out-of-sample, CHOP round-trips 24% / 48% vs quiet 0.5% / 1.7% → this is the fade-vs-ride call.
  • HIGH / LOW band — where the extremes land (~50% empirical coverage). Your fade entry + revert-target zone.
  • expected close displacement — typical net move size (calibrated in aggregate, noisy per single window).

🌆 "Isn't the direction model better in the CEST evening?" — the honest answer

Measured (2026-06-21, 18 time-cells over live rounds): evening 20:00–24:00 CEST on 15m was the single best cell — 61.8% win, Wilson-LB 51.4%, positive 7/8 days — the only cell whose lower bound cleared 50%. But on the more recent window its lower bound fell to 46.3%, and it was partly selection-biased, so it was never promoted — it lives as a shadow observation, and the soft time prior you see is the 🕐 chip above (reversal setups, not direction). The broader truth: direction by hour/day/weekday sits at 45–53% everywhere with every lower bound <50% — there is no clock you can bet by itself. If the evening-15m cell re-proves itself on fresh data, it earns a feature; until then, treat "evening is better" as a hypothesis the data currently declines to confirm.

The honest gate (do NOT skip this): the path engine gives you window-selection + timing + the fade grade. It does not pick which side settles, and it is not profit by itself. A fade only pays if, at the touch, the cheap (losing) share is actually mispriced versus the reach-anchor odds — i.e. it still has to clear fair_value − ask − taker_fee − buffer > required_edge. A BTC-price barrier scenario is not Polymarket P&L. Path features identify events to study; only recorded executable quotes and realized share outcomes can establish expectancy. Until the recorder proves that edge after costs, treat every fade as PAPER.

🧠 The two probability instruments (the betting brain)

Two probabilities answer different questions. They must not be blended into one confidence score:

  • 🎯 P(Hold) — conditional late-state probability. "Price is already $X past the line with S seconds left; will this same side remain ahead?" The current decision-level audit is roughly 93% held for first qualifying 5m/15m entries, but it is still not profit without the market price.
  • 📊 Direction / P(beat) — research context only. "Will the window close above its open?" Current resolved 5m/15m ensemble accuracy is below 50%, so it is not a trustworthy standalone fair-value engine or bet trigger.

The value rule: a paper entry exists only when conservative P(Hold) exceeds the executable ask plus the exact taker fee plus a 3c safety buffer. High win probability at a rich price can still lose money.

🔄 In-window states — opened vs live

A bet locks at window-open, so the big header (direction · grade) is the snapshot at open and stays fixed. The card then tracks the live market in two places below the header — and those, not the header, tell you how the bet is doing:

WhereWhat it tells you
"Now: $… (±$X → UP/DOWN side)"Which side price is on right now, and by how much. If it's on the opposite side of your bet, you're currently losing.

So a header that says UP · Grade C while the live note says "live lean now DOWN" is not a contradiction — it's "you opened UP, the model has since flipped DOWN." The header is the photo at open; the live note is now.

Read the advice box, not the frozen header, once a bet is live.

🌀 Why the lean flips — and why a flipping lean is a SKIP

You'll see a card open UP and minutes later read "live lean now DOWN." That is not the model being broken or indecisive — it's the model being honest about a coin-flip.

What's actually happening

  • On a weak window the model's true answer is NEUTRAL. The market still needs a side, so the card shows whichever way the probability tilts by a hair — 50.4% UP one moment, 49.6% the next.
  • The model re-predicts every few seconds on fresh price/flow. When the signal sits right at 50%, any tiny move pushes the tilt across the line. The "flip" is a near-zero number changing sign — there was never real conviction to abandon.
  • High-conviction calls do NOT flip. A Grade-A 65%-one-side lean is anchored far from 50% and holds steady. Only the weak ones flip.
The rule: a lean that flips is the model telling you "I don't know." That instability is the no-edge signal. If it's flipping, skip it. Stability is the fingerprint of a real signal — bet the calm, conviction leans; ignore the flippers (which is most 5m/15m windows).

📐 Reading the magnitude forecast

The model forecasts how far price typically travels, with a 50% band. The card shows:

📐 Typical drop ≈ $40 (50% band $22–$61) → projects close $62,296 (−$30 vs beat → DOWN resolves)

How to use it

  • It's a TYPICAL move, not a path guarantee. The label is the median for current conditions; a $100+ window lands outside the band ~50% of the time by design. Don't read it as "price will do exactly this."
  • Edge = projected-vs-beat distance. The further the projection clears the line, the safer the directional bet.
  • The band is your risk. If the band's near edge ($22 here) still clears the line, the move is reliably big enough. If the band straddles the line, the magnitude is a coin-flip even when the sign is right → drop to Tier C / skip.
  • Magnitude can disagree with "where it is now." Price can be on the UP side this second while the model expects a $40 drop — a fade. Trust the projection over the current tick only when the lean is stable and high-grade.
What this is NOT (yet): there is no "fair value in cents" / probability on the card. An earlier version showed one, but it was built on this flat typical-move number and was misleadingly precise, so it was removed. A real P(close ≥ beat) only returns once the magnitude estimate itself becomes trustworthy (a future retrain) — and only if it then proves out. No fake precision in the meantime.

🚫 Bet-killers (instant skip, no matter what else looks good)

  • Flipping lean. If it changes UP↔DOWN every minute, the model is at 50/50. Instability = no edge. Skip.
  • Fallback lean. ⚠ WEAK = coin-flip. The price can look like free money; it isn't.
  • CHOP / STRETCH outlook. The model is telling you the closing side is random, or the move can't reach the line. Believe it.
  • TRENDING_UP / HIGH_VOLATILITY regime. The weakest cells in live evidence (~46%) — trends run over mean-reversion leans. (Quiet RANGE / LOW_VOLATILITY regimes are where calls actually clear coin-flip, ~59–63%.)
  • Band straddles the line. Right sign, wrong size — the magnitude is a coin-flip even when the direction isn't.
  • Tiny sample win-rate. "model 100% (2)" means nothing. Wait for n≥100 before trusting the strip.
  • Paying above your edge. The single most common way to lose while being "right" — win-rate must beat the share price.

The one law behind all three: every extra spread crossing and every early profit-take is a transfer to the market maker. The only measured leak in this market is the final ~30 seconds — crossed once, held to settlement (the 📜 frozen rule above).

🧠 Honest math

  • 5-minute BTC direction is mostly noise. Every model family in the ensemble independently lands at ~51% out-of-sample — that's not a broken model, it's the honest ceiling of 5m direction on these inputs. Tuning or bigger models won't move it; only genuinely new information can.
  • The edge lives in the SELECTED slice, not every call. Some cells are real (calm-market 3m/10m have shown ~54–56%). You don't need 55% everywhere — you need it on the few windows you act on: stable model leans + magnitude clearing the line + good regime, and skip the rest.
  • Win-rate is not profit. Profit = win-rate vs share price. Cheaper entry beats more predictions. Always. 60% wins at 65¢ loses money.
  • Fewer bets = higher win-rate. Nobody sustains 70% at 5m. You get a high win-rate by betting the best 5–10% of windows and sitting out the other 90%+. The app is a filter, not an oracle.
  • Until the strip shows model-leans clearing ~55% at n≥100, treat this as PAPER-TRACKING, not sized betting. The model is mid-measurement.
